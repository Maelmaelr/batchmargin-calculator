The method

Revenue tells half the story.

A useful event decision includes every cost caused by attending—not only the ingredients or inventory you sold.

01

Cost the product

Count ingredients, materials, packaging, and other per-unit costs for the products actually sold.

02

Cost the event

Add the booth, card processing, travel, parking, and any fixed cost you would not have incurred otherwise.

03

Price the time

Include every worker-hour for setup, selling, and pack-down at the hourly rate the work needs to earn.

04

Compare the outcome

Net profit, margin, and profit per worker-hour make repeat, reprice, or skip decisions much clearer.

Avoid double-counting labor.

If your product cost already includes production labor, keep it there. Use the calculator's labor field only for setup, selling, and pack-down time—or remove production labor from product cost first.

Small print, plain English

Before you use the result.

Is this accounting or tax advice?

No. It is a planning estimate for comparing selling events. Your accounting treatment, tax deductions, and local rules may differ.

What hourly labor rate should I use?

Use the minimum hourly amount the selling work needs to earn. If you have paid staff, include their loaded hourly cost instead.

Does BatchMargin store my numbers?

No. The calculator runs in this browser tab and does not submit or save your entries.
